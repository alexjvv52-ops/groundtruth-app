# 2. Money cannot lie
**Law.** Money cannot lie. Unpaid is unpaid. Unpriced stays unpriced. No
dual books.
**Why.** A grower can survive a bad week. A grower cannot survive a record
that says the money came in when it did not. Soft money is the failure that
every pretty dashboard hides best, because a total that shows no work looks
finished. Here every figure shows the rows it used, and a figure that the
data cannot support is refused in plain words instead of guessed.
**What it forbids.**
- A "paid" that was never settled. A delivered order stays owed until a
  payment is recorded against that row.
- Silent shortfall. If less arrives than was owed, the difference is
  written off explicitly, as a write-off, on the record.
- Unpriced lines entering the money path. A blank price cannot be recorded
  through the order door, and neither settlement door will settle a line
  with no price.
- Editing or deleting money history. Corrections and voids are new events
  that leave the original visible.
- A second ledger anywhere — in a spreadsheet, on the phone, in an AI's
  summary — that could be read as the real one.
- Pipeline values. A lead, a sample, or a conversation is never money.
**What it still allows.**
- Honest unpaid, honest overdue, honest bad debt — each named as itself.
- Corrections, refunds, and reversals, each as an event with a trail.
- Books: read-only lenses over the same money (cash in, cash out, net,
  write-offs, bad debt), never a place where money is entered.
- Online payment links that reserve capacity only when the payment
  actually confirms, never on a cart or a promise.
**How you can tell.** Health carries money checks on the same severity
engine as everything else: it cannot read calm while money is owed and it
cannot read calm after a failed read. What a tray costs is computed only
when you ask, with the method statement underneath, and is never stored.
