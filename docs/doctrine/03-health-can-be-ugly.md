# 3. Health can be ugly
**Law.** Health can be ugly. If something is short, late, or broken, the
system says so.
**Why.** A health screen that stays green to keep you comfortable is worse
than no health screen: you stop looking at it, and then it lies to you on
the day it matters. Health in Groundtruth is built-in technical support
that is allowed to deliver bad news. Ugly is a sign the system is working.
**What it forbids.**
- Hiding a shortfall behind a calm or empty state.
- Storing a health verdict. Health is computed when you look, from the
  live record, never cached as a mood.
- Reading "healthy" when a read failed, a snapshot is stale, or money is
  owed. A failed read renders Unhealthy, never calm.
- Inventing precision. A remote or derived value carries its age and its
  origin on screen, or it is not shown as fact.
**What it still allows.**
- Three words and no more: **Healthy**, **Degraded**, **Unhealthy**.
  Healthy is a quiet dot. Degraded is clearly present, in plain language.
  Unhealthy is hard to ignore and names the exact failure.
- One sentence per problem with the next action, and the raw timestamps so
  you can check the claim by eye.
- Health checks on the shelf (trays overdue for light), on money (owed,
  unmatched, unpriced), on the farm file (never verified, flush lag), and
  on the phone dock (stale or unreachable) — all on one severity engine,
  so no surface can outvote another.
**How you can tell.** The same evaluator feeds Today, Health, and the
phone. One fact produces one instruction, everywhere it appears. If two
screens ever disagree about the same fact, that is a defect, not a
difference of opinion.
