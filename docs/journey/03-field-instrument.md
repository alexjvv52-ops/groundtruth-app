# The Field Instrument
How a phone was allowed near the farm record without ever becoming a
second writer.
## The problem
The rack is where facts are born — a weight, a count, a tray that should
have moved to light — and the rack is not where the PC is. Every paid tool
in this category solves that by letting the phone write. Groundtruth
refused, because two writers mean two histories. The question became: what
can a phone do that is useful and still cannot invent the farm?
## The answer, in two halves
**Capture.** The phone proposes. A capture at the rack lands in a proposal
ledger on the PC and waits. A person confirms or discards it on the desk —
on Today, beside the other morning work. Only a confirmed proposal becomes
an event. The ledger was the first rack-side fence; a field terminal that
pairs, pushes, and pulls was the second.
**See.** The phone reads the PC's own snapshot. The desk runs a dock: a
small document served on the local network behind an admin token minted on
the PC. The document is a projection of the same status computation that
feeds Today and Health — one snapshot pass, one clock — so the phone shows
exactly what the desk would show, never a phone-side recomputation.
## The rulings that made it honest
Thirteen rulings were signed before the spine was built. The ones a reader
needs:
- **Two ages on the wire, always.** When the snapshot was served, and when
  attention was last evaluated — both stamped by the PC, absolute and
  date-bearing, never derived from the phone's clock or from fetch time.
  A stale snapshot says how stale it is.
- **The same three words.** Healthy, Degraded, Unhealthy. The phone does
  not get a softer vocabulary.
- **Six nodes.** Money, Capacity, Promise, Rack, Phone queue, and Health,
  each with a card title and a sentence composed on the PC. A node with no
  honest source shows no severity rather than an invented one.
- **Clash sentences are PC-composed.** When two cards conflict, the phone
  shows a sentence the desk wrote, never a token the phone assembled.
- **The task mirror links, it does not complete.** Field-safe actions are
  links to existing capture verbs. There is no completion control and no
  new write door.
- **Export carries an authority line.** A diagnosis packet exported from
  the phone says what it is and how old it is, so no reader mistakes it
  for a live writer.
- **Offline-first was narrowed, in writing, to the capture path.** Seeing
  requires being on the local network with the desk running.
- **Auto-pull is visibility-gated with backoff**, and the displayed age
  never comes from the fetch.
## What was declined, on the record
Two proposals were declined rather than deferred, so they do not sit on a
list waiting for a quiet week:
- **A step vocabulary on the wire.** The phone shows full PC sentences
  only. The forced morning loop stays on the desk.
- **TLS on the LAN dock.** A secure context would have forced an origin
  change, token re-entry on every paired phone, new failure sentences, and
  a private-key placement that conflicted with the farm's own backup
  instruction. The native select-all and system copy were accepted as the
  export path instead. An offline See surface remains future work that
  needs its own signature.
## The day it landed
The eleven fences of the spine — honest age and document version, pull,
six nodes, card titles, clash model, edge list, phone queue, task mirror,
diagnosis export, See-to-Act navigation, pull health — landed on
2026-08-23, from tip `c33f9d2` to `465dee0`, after the dock jobs of
08-21 and 08-22 had put every fold the phone would show onto the PC first.
Card faces and the schematic followed the same night; language and return
path corrections the next day.
## What the phone may do today
See the PC's snapshot with its two ages. Capture a proposal. Accept or
discard nothing — that happens on the desk. Export a diagnosis packet with
its authority line. Nothing else, by law.
